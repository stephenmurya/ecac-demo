# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Church leaders and operations staff are the primary demo audience. Future users include organization owners, branch administrators, pastoral-care teams, communications staff, finance teams, ministry leaders, home-cell leaders, volunteers, members, visitors, and platform operators. They work in busy, interruption-heavy environments and need clear next actions rather than technical infrastructure detail.

## Product Purpose

The Church Digital Operations Platform unifies church CRM, engagement, communications, automation, attendance, giving, ministry needs, AI-assisted content, reporting, and administration. The first pilot serves one church while preserving a documented path to safe multi-tenancy. The current deliverable is a leadership-facing static demo, not production architecture.

## Brand Personality

Calm, capable, and trustworthy. The interface should feel operationally mature and human, with church context expressed through useful language rather than religious decoration.

## Anti-references

- Flashy AI dashboards, neon effects, glassmorphism, or decorative gradients.
- Consumer social feeds that obscure operational work.
- Church-themed ornament, stock worship imagery, or sermon-poster aesthetics.
- Dense enterprise software that requires training to locate basic actions.
- Interfaces that make simulated actions look like live payments or messages.

## Design Principles

1. Put attention before analytics: lead with what needs a person today.
2. Make scope and status obvious: organization, branch, workflow state, and responsibility should be legible.
3. Keep people in control: sensitive pastoral, finance, and church-wide communication actions retain human review.
4. Demonstrate the connected system: show how a visitor, automation, communication, and ministry need move between teams.
5. Earn trust through restraint: familiar controls, plain language, visible confirmation, and honest demo boundaries.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for the demo: keyboard-operable controls, visible focus, semantic landmarks, sufficient contrast, reduced-motion support, text alternatives, and status communication that does not depend on color alone. Responsive behavior prioritizes desktop and remains usable on tablets and narrow screens.
